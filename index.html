Still Life with Librarian

Fine, Performing, and Practical Arts

When we make things with our hands, we’re engaging in a research-backed practice: every studio, shop, and kitchen is its own sort of research institution.

Every completed or abandoned project readily offers answers about materials, methods, and the makers who came before us. My goal is to help students articulate the corresponding questions. Library sessions are designed to fit your rooms and your schedule, and each one concludes with something students made or can use.

Two Collaborative Paths

Ask 1

Lost Days

Some days, the workshop can’t run: the paint is drying, the kitchen is closed, a sub is covering, you’re wiped out from being voluntold to participate in an event the night prior. Book any of those days and the library supplies a readymade lesson in your discipline, led by me, in the library or your room.

Ask 2

Planned Units

Some collaborations deserve more time. We can design a unit together around a project you already teach or want to teach. I’ll bring the sources, the research methods, and the citation help.

Now on View

  • Draw It Like the WPA

    Studio Art | Sketch | short

    During the Depression, the WPA’s Federal Art Project paid out-of-work artists to paint American subjects and scenes. Many of these works disappeared into private collections and the sands of time (and, alas, the trash). One branch, the Index of American Design, survives at the National Gallery of Art, which holds its 18,257 watercolors of everyday objects, from weathervanes to quilts to chairs. Students choose an everyday object, sketch it in the same style, and draft a catalog record to make it discoverable.

  • Menu Archaeology

    Culinary | Service | one period

    The New York Public Library holds more than 40,000 restaurant menus dating from the 1840s to the present. Volunteers once transcribed them dish by dish; the library has since retired that searchable site, but the menus themselves remain online. Students will trace a single dish across a century and explain how, when, and why it changed.

  • Mission: Furniture

    Woodshop | Finish | several days

    Gustav Stickley’s family home, Craftsman Farms, is open for tours in Parsippany. Students study his furniture and his writing; design and build a small piece in his spirit; and document it museum-style with a maker’s statement and a catalog record.

How It Works

  • Your room or mine? I’ll come to your studio, shop, or kitchen, or you can bring your class to the library, where there’s room to spread out and real books that students just might want to read.
  • Planning an absence? Hit Frontline, then talk to me.
  • You handle the craft; I handle the research. Everything comes readymade with nothing to grade (unless you want to grade it).

Why It Counts

  • New Jersey’s administrative code calls for information literacy delivered in partnership by school library media specialists and classroom educators.
  • New Jersey’s arts standards ask students to relate artistic ideas and works to their societal, cultural, and historical contexts. Every sample here is rooted in that connection.

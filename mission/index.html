The Assignment

Gustav Stickley wanted furniture to telegraph how it was made. He favored oak, plain lines, and joints left in plain sight. His family home, Craftsman Farms, still stands in Parsippany. We’ll study his work and ideas, then design and build a small piece in his spirit, and conclude by documenting the pieces and process the way a museum would.

Origins

  • Als Ik KanStickley marked his furniture with a joiner’s compass and a Flemish motto, usually translated as “to the best of my ability.” What would your mark say?
  • Joints in plain sightHis tables and chairs leave their mortise-and-tenon joints exposed. Why emphasize the structure rather than concealing it?
  • The CraftsmanHis monthly magazine (1901 to 1916) printed essays, furniture designs, and house plans (subscribers could send away for a set of house plans annually). He was selling something other than furniture and periodical subscriptions. What was it?
  • Craftsman FarmsStickley bought land in Morris Plains (now Parsippany) for a boys’ farm school that never opened. His family lived there instead. Which of his ideals seem embodied in this place?
  • “Mission”The public called his work “Mission” furniture, and imitators borrowed the label. Who gets credit for an aesthetic or style?
  • Ruskin and MorrisThe English critics John Ruskin and William Morris blamed factories for shoddy goods. Stickley ran a large workshop in Eastwood, New York. Did he agree with them?

How This Works

  1. Get your bearings. Read about Stickley and the Arts and Crafts movement in Britannica School or Credo Source; search the open web if you’re so inclined.
  2. Dig deeper! Read Stickley’s own words in The Craftsman. Study his furniture in museum collections and Britannica ImageQuest. Search JSTOR for scholarship.
  3. Design! Draw your piece to scale. Name the joint(s) you will use and explain why.
  4. Build! Follow your teacher’s shop procedures.
  5. Document. Write your maker’s statement and catalog record. Build your citations in NoodleTools (MLA).

The Maker’s Statement

One page (~250 words): what you built, which of Stickley’s principles it borrows, what you changed and why, and at least two sources. Add a catalog record: title, maker, date, materials, dimensions, and joinery.
